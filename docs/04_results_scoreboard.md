Three methods reach 8/8 and 380/380: xGT, press resistance and pitch control.
Choosing between those three is argued in `05_why_xgt_won.md`.

## Method by method

**01 — Expected Goal Threat.** The first formulation, with no way to lose the ball,
is degenerate: on a connected map every possession eventually arrives, so every city
scores 1 and `h` is 0 everywhere. Adding a pass-completion probability fixes that,
but the resulting heuristic sits about 1.8× above true cost and A\* turns greedy —
287 of 380 routes optimal. The fix was not a scale factor but a change to the model:
let the passer prefer receivers who are themselves threatening. With that, 380/380.

**02 — Progressive passing.** Rewarding the threat a pass gains, rather than the
threat a city has. Ranks well (0.990) and guides well (370/380), but it is built on
top of xGT and inherits its Arad/Oradea blind spot, and the values are hard to read
as "remaining cost" — cities next to the goal come out very low and the middle of
the map flattens out. Better as an ingredient than as the answer.

**03 — Press resistance.** Add up the quality of every outlet instead of averaging
over them. The difference sounds cosmetic and is not: averaging lets weak outlets
dilute a well-connected city, adding lets them only help. That flips the method from
over- to under-estimating, which is why it reaches 380/380 where plain xGT with the
same parameters does not. The closest thing to a rival that came out of the brief.

**04 — Route redundancy.** Max-flow from city to goal with capacity 1/w. It fails
the first sanity pair outright: Giurgiu, one road from Bucharest, scores worse than
Pitesti, because one narrow road carries less flow than three wider ones. Capacity
measures how wide the corridors are and nothing about how long they are. Kept
because it is the clearest example in the set of a structurally interesting idea
that simply cannot express distance.

**05 — Packing / line breaking.** Bridges and cut vertices as defensive lines to
break. The western half of the map is 2-connected, so no single road or city
separates it from Bucharest, and twelve cities score 0. It is a real property of the
graph — it just happens to be a property that only exists in the eastern tail.

**06 — Pitch control.** Influence spreads out from the goal and fades with road cost
and with every hand-over. Two parameters, a five-line iteration, and the best raw
numbers in the table. It was the runner-up, and the argument against it is about the
model rather than the measurements — see the next page.

**07 — Goal-seeded centrality.** PageRank restarted at the goal. It distinguishes
hubs from dead ends nicely, which is exactly what makes it wrong here: Giurgiu is a
dead end one road from Bucharest, so the random walk rarely visits it and the
heuristic calls it far away.

**08 — Combined model.** The brief's preferred shape: threat × support × progression.
On this map the three ingredients agree with each other so strongly that multiplying
them mostly rescales xGT, and since both extra factors are ≤ 1 the rescaling pushes
`h` further above true cost. Ranking stays excellent, A\* gets worse (333/380). A
negative result we did not expect.

**09 — Expected time to goal (ours).** Mean time for a possession to arrive,
averaged over every passing sequence the model might produce. Already in road-cost
units, so no conversion needed — which was the appeal. The problem is intrinsic:
mean first-passage time is dominated by wandering unless the walk is strongly
goal-directed, and a strongly goal-directed walk is a shortest-path computation
wearing a hat. There is no setting where it is both football-random and good at
guiding A\*.

**10 — Turnover risk (ours).** Same family: expected cost paid before either
reaching the goal or losing the ball, with a penalty per turnover taken from the
mean road cost. Better than 09 because the penalty scales with the number of passes
rather than with how far the ball wanders. Still 331/380. Worth recording that the
turnover penalty barely matters: setting it to zero changes nothing in the ranking,
because the restart term already dominates.
